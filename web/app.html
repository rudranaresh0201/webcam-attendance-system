      How it decided

      1. Crop and even out. The face is cut out, resized to 200×200 and its contrast evened out, so a dim room and a bright room look alike.
      2. Describe the texture. Each pixel is compared with its 8 neighbours: brighter or darker? Those 8 answers make a code for spots, edges and corners.
      3. Compare. The codes are counted in each square of an 8×8 grid and compared with every enrolled photo. Brighter squares differ more; eyes and brows count most.
      4. Decide. It must be close to one student and clearly closer to them than to anyone else, including a hidden crowd of 192 strangers, in 5 of the last 8 frames.

      About

      This page runs FaceRoll's Python code (OpenCV, NumPy) in your browser through WebAssembly. It is the same pipeline as the desktop app: Haar + LBP face detection, uniform LBP texture histograms with learned weights, and a runner-up test against other students and a hidden crowd.
