FaceRoll

Takes classroom attendance from a laptop webcam, using classical image processing only. No neural networks: every step is something you can look at.

How it works

Each camera frame goes through the same four steps. You can switch the live video to any of these intermediate images inside the app.

  1. Clean up

    Slight blur to remove grain, grey, then contrast evened out tile by tile (CLAHE) so a dim corner looks like a bright one.

  2. Find faces

    Two face finders run: Haar (light and dark blocks, like eyes darker than cheeks) and an LBP cascade (texture). A face counts when they agree.

  3. Describe texture

    Every pixel is compared with its 8 neighbours: brighter or darker? Those 8 answers form a code. Codes are counted in an 8×8 grid over the face.

  4. Decide

    The counts are compared with each student's photos. A face is accepted only when it is close to one student and clearly closer to them than to anyone else.

The problems, and how we fixed them

The textbook version (OpenCV's LBPH recogniser with a distance threshold) accepted 91% of strangers as a registered student in our first trial. Each fix below targets one cause and was measured on its own.

Shelves and curtains detected as faces

Two detectors vote

Haar and LBP features are fooled by different backgrounds, so a box is kept when both agree, or when one is backed by a pair of eyes.

wrong boxes per frame 0.31 → 0.10
Hair and background count as much as the eyes

Learned weights for each square

We measured how well each square of the face tells people apart, on people never used for testing. The eyes and brows ended up weighted about 3×, the corners about 0.

right person picked 57% → 67%
Strangers matched to the nearest student

The runner-up test

A real student is much closer to their own photos than to anyone else's. A stranger is about equally far from everyone. So the best match has to beat the second best by a clear margin.

strangers kept out, students missed 88% → 66%
With one student, everyone "looks like" them

A hidden crowd

192 faces of people who are not in the class sit silently in the gallery (stored as texture counts, not photos). A stranger usually lands closer to some crowd face and is rejected.

strangers accepted, 1 student: 55% → 0.3%
Masks, sunglasses, a hand on the cheek

Ignore the worst squares

The distance skips the 20% of squares that match worst, so covering part of the face can only spoil part of the comparison.

masked face: never recognised → 38% of frames
Someone holds up a photo

Ask for a blink

The Haar eye detector only fires on open eyes. With the blink check on, a face is credited only after its eyes close and reopen.

printed photos never credited

Run it yourself

Try it live in your browser: the same Python code runs inside the page (WebAssembly), with your webcam. Nothing is uploaded; enrolments and registers stay in your browser.

Or run the full desktop version. You need Python 3.10–3.12 and a webcam. Face photos and attendance sheets stay on your laptop.

git clone https://github.com/rudranaresh0201/webcam-attendance-system
cd webcam-attendance-system
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate        # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python app.py                    # then open http://127.0.0.1:8000

Enrol yourself on the Enrol tab (30 photos, about 10 seconds), press Start attendance, and look at the camera. The register is saved as records/attendance_YYYY-MM-DD.csv.

Limits

  • Frontal faces only. Both face finders are trained on faces looking at the camera; a student has to face it at some point.
  • It is cautious. It would rather miss a student than mark a stranger. On news photos taken years apart it misses many; on a webcam with recent enrolment it is far better. Re-enrol in the room's usual light if you get missed.
  • The blink check stops photos, not videos. A phone playing a video of a student could pass.
