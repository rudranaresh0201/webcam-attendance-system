      How it decided

      1. Crop and even out. The face is cut out, resized to 200×200 and its contrast evened out, so a dim room and a bright room look alike.
      2. Describe the texture. Each pixel is compared with its 8 neighbours: brighter or darker? Those 8 yes/no answers make a code for spots, edges and corners.
      3. Compare. The face is split into an 8×8 grid and the codes in each square are counted. Each square is compared with the enrolled photos. Brighter squares differ more. Eyes and brows count most.
      4. Decide. It must be close to one student and clearly closer to them than to anyone else, in 5 of the last 8 frames. One bad frame (a blink, a glance down) doesn't reset it.
      The maths
      d = 1Σ w Σj wj Σb (Sjb − Mjb)²Sjb + Mjb

      S is the live face and M an enrolled photo. j runs over the 128 grid squares (64 squares at two radii) and b over the 59 texture codes. wj is how useful each square is, learned from data. A face is accepted when d ≤ τd and dclosest / dsecond ≤ τr.
